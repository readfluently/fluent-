Water Cycle Concepts

The water cycle is the continuous movement of water between the earth’s surface and the atmosphere. Water changes form and moves from one place to another again and again.

Evaporation

Liquid water is heated by the sun and changes into water vapour that rises into the air.

Condensation

Water vapour cools high in the atmosphere and changes into tiny droplets that form clouds.

Precipitation

When droplets in clouds become heavy, water falls back to earth as rain, snow, or hail.

Collection and movement

Water gathers in rivers, lakes, dams, and oceans, and some moves through soil, plants, and streams.

What is the water cycle?

The water cycle is the continuous movement of water between the earth’s surface and the atmosphere. Water does not stay in one place. It moves through air, land, rivers, lakes, oceans, plants, and clouds.

The cycle is driven mainly by the sun’s heat. Water evaporates into the air, condenses to form clouds, falls as precipitation, and then collects again on land and in water bodies. Some water also moves through soil and plants.

Energy source

The sun provides the heat energy that starts evaporation.

In the atmosphere

Water vapour cools and forms clouds by condensation.

Back to earth

Water returns through precipitation such as rain, snow, or hail.

Continuous process

Water keeps moving, so the cycle repeats again and again.
